# Claim ledger: prospective mouse learning forecasts

29 October 2026. Evidence map for [manuscript.md](manuscript.md), an unpublished working draft. This ledger does not change historical protocols, endpoints, freezes or validation outcomes. Paths below are relative to this directory. “Established” means supported for the specified recipe and sample, not universal biological truth.

## Scope and closest prior

The proposed contribution is a frozen across-animal, prefix-limited acquisition forecast with a generated adaptive trainer and prospective comparison against empirical growth in previously unused source animals. It is **not** the first held-out behavioral prediction, first generated learning trajectory, first evaluation using generated choices, or completed real cross-policy transport test.

The closest sources are Liu, Geadah and Pillow's [neural learning-rule paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04661v2), including conditional held-out prediction and recurrent architecture; Geadah and Pillow's [de novo learning-rule paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621722/), including separate curriculum fits and Appendix Figure 9's sampled-decision predictive evaluation; and Pan-Vazquez et al.'s [neural acquisition predictors](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.09.045). The [new prior-work clarification](PRIOR_UPDATE.md) supersedes any exhaustive reading of the historical campaign's narrower Section 2.3 discussion. In Figure 9, simulated trajectories draw decisions at each step and their emissions are scored at observed choices. This is a direct counterexample to “all prior evaluation used actual intervening choices.” The exact combination tested here has not received an exhaustive priority assessment.

## Claims excluded from the draft

- A unique synaptic learning rule, a uniquely identified session-retention mechanism, or intrinsic unpredictability of individual learning.
- Equivalence of learned rules and growth, or proof that true population bias exceeds 5 pp.
- Causal evidence that revealing choices/rewards alone explains the tracking/autonomous gap; the actual stimulus and adaptive-history regimes also differ.
- A new claim that generative-choice prediction itself is novel, or that the source literature has no autonomous simulations.
- Measured benefit of fixed versus adaptive curricula, a completed real external transport test, or permission to consume the external cohort based on a diagnostic.
- Treating the future-only 0.8 perturbation or same-cohort actual-history diagnostic as a replacement model that passed prospective validation.
- General biological validity inferred solely from exact software replay, or a cross-domain mechanism inferred from unrelated evaluator campaigns.
