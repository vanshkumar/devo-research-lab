## What would advance the persistent question

A useful result would predict quantitative acquisition trajectories under a changed teacher using learning dynamics inferred only from the source training policy. The comparison must exceed teacher-only stimulus selection and sensory/difficulty-aware exposure growth. Generating one-step probabilities on the actual future history, replaying the teacher, or reproducing published faster MT training would not establish the intended autonomous predictive capability.

The common teacher's factors cancel in joint one-step log-score comparisons on a fixed observed history. A future design therefore needs frozen autonomous trajectory predictions, with separate stimulus-mixture, omission and difficulty-specific response reporting. Count-based trajectory scores must not obscure whether improvement comes only from easier served stimuli. A fixed common-difficulty probe without actual support is a model prediction, not observed validation. Candidate predictive scores, material advantage and uncertainty require known-effect/null calibration before new scored futures; no metric or threshold is selected here.
