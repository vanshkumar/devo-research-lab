## Closest claims and comparison

Wilson et al. measure horizon effects on the **first** free choice and use a Bellman calculation as an optimal reference. The saved one-step policy values an extra sample assuming greedy use of the new posterior for the remaining choices. The fitted first-choice interpolation and the one-step prescription are two different extensions. A result for either would leave the published first-choice inference intact. Feng et al.'s first-choice drift-diffusion analysis relies on choice and RT. It does not give a prospective later-choice prescription. A forced-origin joint forecast with generated feedback therefore tests a different decision. It can determine whether a fixed policy supplies adequate action and reward trajectories when the downstream feedback path is unknown.
