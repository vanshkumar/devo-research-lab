# The six-response assay mostly identifies the response model without assignment labels

**November 4, 2026 ET. Close the 40% versus 55% medium-contrast design for curriculum-forecast discrimination at 240 animals. Bank the scoped design result.** Discarding assignment labels still gave 99.78–100% power to distinguish the two recurrent prescriptions from the history-responsive psychometric tracker in the candidate assay. A separate calibration that preserves every signed arm difference in path probability still capped power below .49, even with an oracle that reveals the archived prefix and grants the most favorable four-site panel under a broad range of common response profiles. The engineered order adds little to the assignment comparison over either length-matched control. ## Evidence with and without assignment

I analyzed the hash-verified saved laws for 64 archived prefix contexts in nine sites, two scenarios and 729 ternary paths per assay. Each animal contributes one path. Both arms have probability .5. At each conditioning level, let $M_m(y)=[P_m(y\mid A=0)+P_m(y\mid A=1)]/2$ for model $m$. The KL divergence of $(Y,A)$ splits exactly into the KL divergence between models' path marginals $M_m(Y)$ and the expected KL divergence between their conditional assignment laws $P_m(A\mid Y)$. The path marginal is itself a postassignment distribution, and its separation can reflect effects averaged over arms. The target here is the predicted *difference* between assignments. Conditional assignment odds can also differ when additive effects agree. The script verifies that case with two explicit binary laws. [Independent reduction](contrast_audit.py)

In the nominal equal-site mixture, the unchanged and session GRU comparisons had 50.05/52.35 and 35.89/39.59 total joint nats at $N=240$ in the two KL directions. The added label terms were only .00389/.00640 and .00204/.00227 nats. Under the declared engagement stress they were .00153–.00385 nats. Giving the test site labels or exact prefix contexts exposes some cancellation in the mixture. Even with prefix identity the largest candidate label term was .101 nat, and across the pooled, site and prefix analyses at least 99.89% of raw candidate KL was available without assignment. A separate path-only likelihood-ratio test used 24,000 simulated cohorts per calibration or evaluation distribution. Its observed sizes were .0471–.0525, with power 1.000 in both nominal directions for each actor and at least .99779 under stress. [Raw KL and label-free decisions](CONTRAST_AUDIT.json)

## Preserve the arm effects exactly and vary the common path law

The author holds assignment odds contrasts under a specific nested midpoint calibration. I set $d_m(y)=[P_m(y\mid 1)-P_m(y\mid 0)]/2$ and give each model calibrated arms $B(y)-d_m(y)$ and $B(y)+d_m(y)$. The common $B$ can vary by site or known prefix. This preserves the additive effect for every path event or score, including the first response, omissions, order-sensitive events and cross-slot moments. The largest equal-site average shift from either original path marginal to its feasibility-adjusted anchor was 1.07 percentage points of total variation, across candidate scenarios and conditioning levels. The intervening convex hull covers every mixture of those two anchors, with an independent mixture weight for each stratum. Joint convexity bounds each directional KL by its maximum at the endpoints. [Independent calibration and checks](contrast_audit.py)

I also bounded a wider set without choosing the shape of $B$. Require $B(y)\ge\max_m |d_m(y)| + .25 M_{\rm mid}(y)$, where $M_{\rm mid}$ is the original midpoint. The remaining probability mass can move arbitrarily. Every calibrated arm probability is then at least $.25M_{\rm mid}(y)$, so the chi-squared upper bound on joint KL is at most $\sum_y [d_1(y)-d_2(y)]^2/[.25M_{\rm mid}(y)]$ in either direction. At size .05 and power .80, data processing through the rejection event requires 1.9064 nats from alternative to null and 1.3416 in reverse. Summing over 240 independent learners gives the ceilings below. These are upper bounds for the saved laws under the stated calibration classes, with the point laws and context distribution known to the decision rule. The wider class excludes profiles that make some simulated paths vanishingly rare relative to the midpoint. A particular $B$, the .25 floor and the stability of additive arm effects are sensitivity assumptions. The ordinary-training data leave all three unverified for deployment. ## What the six slots buy

In nominal conditions the candidate raised raw joint KL by 14–18% relative to the ordinary sequence, depending on the model and direction. The corresponding site-known anchor-hull ceilings were .111/.104 for the candidate, .110/.103 for ordinary, and .111/.103 for the composition-matched reorder. The ordinary sequence slightly exceeded the candidate ceilings in the engagement stress. The author's separate estimates of actual power in its matched laws also fell far short of the prespecified .10 gain and .80 absolute floor. First-response ceilings were .072–.078 with site known across the models and scenarios. The first-response laws agree across all three schedules to $9\times10^{-16}$ and measure the immediate assigned effect before the assay's first feedback. The six-slot law reflects further learning and feedback. [Independent comparisons](CONTRAST_AUDIT.json) · [Frozen gate and author's decision tests](../../tasks/acquisition-reactive-sequence-2026-11-04/SEQUENCE_PLAN.md) · [Screen](../../tasks/acquisition-reactive-sequence-2026-11-04/SCREEN.json)

The candidate's nominal high-minus-low mean correctness gaps were +.071 pp and +.064 pp for the GRUs and −.046 pp for the tracker. The six-slot mean's modeled 95% half-width at 240 was 4.25–4.95 pp, essentially the same as ordinary's 4.30–4.96 pp. First-response half-width was 11.70–12.18 pp. The candidate mean's variance was 0.74–1.14 times the independent-slot calculation with the same marginal accuracies, depending on the nominal model. Collapsing the path to the correctness count retains 37–48% of the nominal calibrated candidate KL at the projected midpoint, or 17–32% under stress. The full path carries information that the mean discards, but the absolute residual is too small. For an independently justified study of a large cumulative performance effect, the ordinary sequence buys almost the same precision. A precise six-slot null could bound large cumulative effects and would leave the modeled curriculum differences unresolved. [Dependent-path precision and count reduction](CONTRAST_AUDIT.json)

## Provenance and investment

On consumed ordinary training, the tracker was fitted on other sites for each fold, then followed each held-site animal's legitimate prefix and past responses. It reduced the six-slot correct residual variance ratio to 1.02 and had equal-site loss .529 versus .514–.515 for the recurrent laws. The shared omission law still had variance ratios .82 and 1.53 in the two development strata. The archived recurrent optimizer used 62 animals, checkpoint selection evaluated 11 of 12 allocated animals and session retention scored 46 complete histories using the same underlying weights. The roster has no animal overlap, but optimizer and retention sites cover 35 of the 64 current contexts. On Hofer and Witten, the two sites absent from the recurrent optimizer, the GRUs still had lower ordinary forecast loss than the tracker. Restricting the simulated candidate to those sites capped anchor-hull power at .098 with site known and .192 with archived prefixes known. This is a sensitivity on two consumed sites with 29 contexts. The ordinary calibration supports the component stimuli, contingent feedback and a strong simple rival. The exact candidate occurred in zero of 25,075 eligible ordinary windows, and the ordinary and reordered sequences occurred 25 and five times. None certifies the fixed reward volume and assignment chronology or validates arm-specific transport. Melinscak and Bach are the closest methods precedent. They optimize cue–outcome schedules in associative-learning simulations against canonical designs, fit alternatives, and explicitly include different mappings from latent learning to response. Their utility is correct model identification. López-Fidalgo, Tommasi and Trandafir already use KL for non-normal model discrimination, and Dette, Melas and Shpilev study sensitivity and robust design over model parameters. The earlier one-response lab review already held additive effects at a common accuracy. What this review adds is the quantitative failure of near-perfect *joint* model identification as a purchasing criterion for the particular randomized curriculum, across many common path profiles and even after revealing the prefix. The six-slot sequence can tighten a cumulative effect estimate while delivering almost no leverage on which model forecasts the assigned difference. [Melinscak and Bach](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007593) · [López-Fidalgo et al.](https://air.unimi.it/handle/2434/25237) · [Dette et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.4652) · [One-response review](../acquisition-probe-investment-2026-11-04/REVIEW.md)

The design would consume about 576,000 preprobe opportunities before shaping and attrition. The strongest reversal would be an independent, eligible assigned-dose and common-probe object that constrains arm-specific feedback and engagement at the relevant stage and either supports a materially larger forecast contrast against the tracker or justifies a narrower observation calibration that makes such a contrast identifiable. A new candidate would need to recover strong bidirectional contrast discrimination against both GRUs, improve on both matched controls, and survive realistic site and engagement variation at a feasible cohort size. The saved point laws and consumed ordinary training do not supply such an object. Close this recipe and preserve the biological question.
