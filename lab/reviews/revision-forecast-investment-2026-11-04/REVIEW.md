# ArgRewrite V.2 revision forecast investment review

**November 4, 2026 · Independent design review · Close this comparison for main funding.**

A held-writer improvement on Draft 3 prediction could show that a particular encoding of the first revision helps forecast a score on the same essay under the original protocol. The closest primary studies address same-transition grade change and the effect of randomized revision interfaces. Even a clear forecasting gain would leave both those results intact and would leave the choice of interface or additional help unanswered. I found no documented consequential assessment decision that uses a forecast at this point, and no directly opposed prior forecast claim with a meaningful effect size to rule out. This is a judgment about the proposed comparison's payoff and design. Its predictive effect remains unknown.

## Closest primary evidence

| Primary study | What it actually tests and what this comparison could change |
| --- | --- |
| Cromp and Litman, EDM workshop 2021, §§3–5 | **Closest same-data forecasting comparator.** Uses ArgRewrite V.2 Drafts 1 and 2 and examines how TF-IDF cosine similarity and revision-purpose counts relate to grade change in that very pair. The authors favor inexpensive similarity for capturing overall/content-related change and discuss the cost of richer annotation. They explicitly acknowledge room to improve as a limitation. Since the features use both drafts of the scored transition, this result establishes a contemporaneous association, with no estimate of incremental Draft 3 forecast value. A Draft 3 result, in either direction, would answer a new time-indexed question while leaving that finding intact. [Primary paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3051/UGR_6.pdf) |
| Afrin et al., CHI 2021, §§4, 6–8 | **Closest actual decision on this cohort.** Which interface to offer for the next revision? Four conditions were assigned after Draft 2. The article favors detailed sentence-level categorization. Its favorable pairwise writing-score comparison uses Draft 1→3 normalized gain, which includes pre-interface change and weights the final outcome by Draft 1 headroom. Randomization can identify an assignment effect on that composite, but the narrower Draft 2→3 tests found no significant pairwise difference. This limits the broad claim and leaves substantial uncertainty. A pooled prognosis of Draft 3 by prior uptake addresses a different estimand. [Proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445683) · [Full author text](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.07018#S6) |
| Afrin and Litman, AIED 2019, §§2, 4 | Revision-derived editor roles were related to a postrevision essay score conditional on the prerevision score. The role features include edits in the interval that produced the scored product. This establishes the closest role/history precedent and makes edit counts, purposes and positions meaningful comparators. It does not establish that a role learned from an already completed edit interval forecasts a later one. [Primary paper](https://people.cs.pitt.edu/~litman/AIED19.pdf) |
| Afrin et al., BEA 2020, and Wu and Schunn, AERJ 2021 | Afrin et al. link evidence/reasoning revision annotations to holistic improvement aligned with feedback over the observed revision interval. Wu and Schunn examine whether peer-feedback activity and revision relate to later writing quality, and use the first draft of a separate writing task after one week as the learning outcome. The former gives a feedback-specific contemporaneous precedent. The latter is a closer benchmark for learning beyond the revised product and measures an outcome V.2 lacks. [Feedback-aligned revision study](https://aclanthology.org/2020.bea-1.7/) · [Separate-task methods and results](https://peerceptiv.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/WuSchunnAERj-learning.pdf) |

Afrin and Litman (2018) and Liu et al. (2023) classify the quality of an already observed edit, in the latter case with argument context. Their annotation/prediction results have a different target and information cutoff. The relevant distinction here is whether the **already completed** response to Draft 1 feedback forecasts the **next** transition after controlling for the present product and ordinary history. The prior screen was targeted, so it supports this distinction without establishing priority for the exact proposed forecast. [2018 paper](https://aclanthology.org/W18-0528/) · [2023 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.bea-1.24/)

## Forecast time and measurement

1. **Immediately after Draft 2 submission.** Drafts 1 and 2 and the personalized feedback actually emailed before Draft 2 are legitimate source objects for the study staff. A draft diff, edit quantities and feedback alignment estimated from those objects can be computed at this time. A score for Draft 1 appears in the collection schematic and is described as withheld from writers. The paper describes feedback aligned to the later scoring criteria, and the companion study reports study ratings for all three drafts. Neither establishes timestamps for the released ratings or that the released Draft 1 score is the same score as in the schematic. Draft 2's released score and the observed score gain therefore belong in a labeled retrospective benchmark until provenance certifies an available rating or a feasible independent contemporaneous scoring procedure. A text-derived current-quality baseline is appropriate for the actual submission-time forecast. [Corpus protocol §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01677#S2.SS2) · [Companion protocol §§4, 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.07018#S4)
2. **After assignment, before interface exposure.** A later cutoff can admit the assigned arm and original Rev12 annotations if their versions are verified as available then. The companion describes manually correcting the text alignment and purpose classifications for the interface. This is expert labeling of what an edit appears to do. A feedback-linked response measure needs a defensible mapping from the actual individualized feedback to the changes and still-unresolved issues. An edit purpose is no direct measure of whether the author acted on the email. A model at submission must instead predict under the randomized policy or marginalize over it using known assignment probabilities. Before-display forecasts may condition on the assigned arm but answer a different operational question. [Corpus protocol §§2.2, 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01677#S2.SS2) · [Companion protocol §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.07018#S4)
3. **During or after the lab session.** The companion reports verification interactions in B–D before and after final revision, and no verification in A. An undifferentiated release field for agreement is unsafe as a predictor without event-level timing. Treat absence by design separately from missing observations. Rev23, Draft 3, poststudy surveys and any updated postrevision annotations are future information. Public release metadata describes the files, including 86 documents per draft and paired annotations grouped by interface. It does not certify the row join, completeness of scores and feedback by learner, or isolation of development and evaluation cells in the archived score workbook. [Release readme](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/omidkashefi/ArgRewrite/main/dataset/readme.txt) · [Companion protocol §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.07018#S4) · [Prior pinned layout and exposure manifest](../../tasks/observed-revision-corrective-judgments-2026-11-02/SOURCE_MANIFEST.json)
4. **Outcome meaning.** The companion reports two experienced researchers independently scoring drafts with a common 10-item rubric (1–4 per item), averaged for a total on the 10–40 scale, and published quadratic weighted kappa of .537. This supports nominal commensurability of study scores across drafts. The available methods do not establish rater overlap with the feedback authors, randomization of grading order, blind separation across drafts/conditions, time of scoring, or invariance of repeated measurements. QWK does not identify the reliability of individual score gains. The shared feedback/rating rubric and same text create common signal and possible correlated errors even across raters. A source-specific study-score forecast and a forecast of independently judged new-task performance are different endpoints. [Companion scoring §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.07018#S6) · [Same-release scoring description, §3.2](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3051/UGR_6.pdf)

## What a valid forecast could discriminate

Use a fixed, low-dimensional comparison on held writers with equal input privileges, same source policy, and paired loss on the **Draft 3 score**. Give the baseline cohort-level progress, a calibrated persistence/shrinkage forecast from current quality, prior quality or gain where legitimately available, ordinary draft structure and length, rubric-specific deficits or headroom, feedback quantity/topic/severity and generic edit sizes, types, and TF-IDF similarity. The feature under test should isolate **correspondence to the actual feedback** from generic changes and current remaining problems. A feedback-mismatch diagnostic should preserve rubric targets, opportunity and initial quality as far as feasible. It is a check for apparent matching gains from common rubric language. Splits, fitting, scaling, feedback alignment choices, and any feature discovery must be grouped by writer and developed without evaluation outcomes. If using external pretrained models or published aggregates, record training/exposure uncertainty.

A positive result still has a simple explanation. In a stylized static-quality model, let all four observations (the three scores and a response summary) be noisy measurements of one stable essay-specific quality, with independent unit-variance normal errors and unit-variance latent quality. Predicting the third score from the first two gives conditional MSE 4/3. Adding the response summary lowers it to 5/4, a 6.25% reduction with zero learning dynamics. The signal could similarly track unresolved essay weaknesses, effort, assessor preferences, or headroom. If the response summary is a deterministic encoding of the two texts and the actual feedback, it adds no information conditional on those *complete* inputs. Better performance against practical baselines would measure the value of the encoding or extra expert judgment. One response to one teacher on one essay cannot separate a stable writer trait from properties of that essay or its feedback opportunities. Randomized interface assignment supports an interface contrast given clean preassignment covariates, but does not randomize prior uptake. For gain targets, independent rating errors alone contribute \(-\operatorname{Var}(e_2)\) to the covariance between \(S_2-S_1\) and \(S_3-S_2\). This can mimic reversal or diminishing responsiveness. Normalizing by headroom also changes the weighting of errors near the ceiling.

## Independent support and the decision it could buy

There is one eligible forecast per writer at most. All 86 authors are from one recruited group, writing one common prompt. The 258 drafts and thousands of edits cannot expand the denominator, and the four arm sizes are 20/22/22/22. Evaluating only explicit feedback matches, complete records, or participants with verification may select easier-to-map edits, particular weakness patterns or treatment arms. A scoped result would apply to the eligible writers and original protocol mixture. A pooled split is incapable of establishing performance on a new assignment, cohort or assessor. [Corpus sample §§2.1–2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01677#S2) · [Interface arm counts, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.07018#S4)

For independently frozen predictors, let \(D_i\) be each evaluation writer's paired loss improvement and \(\sigma_D\) its SD. Under ideal independent normal approximations, a 95% interval has half-width \(1.96\sigma_D/\sqrt{n}\), and about 80% power against zero requires a true mean improvement of \(2.80\sigma_D/\sqrt n\) in a two-sided test. I evaluated the formulas as sensitivity scenarios.

| Available evaluation writers | 95% half-width / \(\sigma_D\) | Approximate 80%-power mean / \(\sigma_D\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| All 86, assuming external development and eligibility | 0.211 | 0.302 |
| 43, illustrative split | 0.299 | 0.427 |
| 26, illustrative held-out evaluation | 0.384 | 0.549 |
| 20–22, single full arm with no development split | 0.418–0.438 | 0.597–0.626 |

Even a fixed 20-versus-22 arm mean contrast has an ideal 80%-power effect of about 0.87 residual SD before adjustment for multiple comparisons. Fitted heterogeneity, small support for a response subgroup, rater dependence and outcome-driven tuning make interpretation harder. Cross-validation can estimate internal performance but its overlapping training folds do not yield independently frozen confirmation or a second population. These are analytic scales, with no data-derived variance, materiality margin, verified usable \(n\), or power certification. The data could plausibly separate a large pooled prediction effect from noise. A weak estimated gain or an interval crossing zero would be poor evidence that response history adds too little to matter.

The existing interface decision requires estimating differences in outcomes **under competing assigned interfaces**, potentially by a preassignment response profile. High predicted need for support and high predicted benefit from support can occur in different learners. The four-arm design provides some identification for a prespecified interaction, but the proposed prediction does not estimate it and the sparse independent support gives little basis for a tailored policy. A teacher-referral decision would also require an actual action, its consequences and a decision-relevant error or value margin. I found these absent in the verified protocol. The alternative research decision of paying to collect feedback-response annotations would need a planned downstream use with a credible benefit/cost or scientific margin. Cromp and Litman's documented tradeoff concerns contemporaneous grade-change proxies, for which this forecast offers no direct counterexample.

**Evidence that would reverse the close.** Identify an existing assessment action or an explicit prospective claim about the value of a response record beyond current quality and ordinary history. Establish which result would change that decision and by how much in its own units. Then verify the original joins, separate release of eligible outcomes, contemporaneous predictor availability, and an independently interpretable score. For a forecast, require a frozen paired comparison against the above controls, with enough genuinely held-out writers and rating evidence to resolve that margin. For individualized interface choice, the relevant new evidence is a credible prespecified arm-by-preassignment response contrast with sufficient overlap and precision and a defined decision criterion. To interpret the signal as acquisition rather than persistent same-essay quality, require an independently scored later task or probe. Simply verifying the existing workbook or obtaining an internally positive pilot would leave the scientific and decision objections in place.

## Scope and provenance

I read the two ArgRewrite papers, named primary comparators and release metadata, with no downloads or reads of corpus files and no access to the parallel investigator's results. Web excerpts incidentally exposed already published aggregate ratings/gains and descriptive results for this cohort, and examples printed in the papers. No individual score, full draft or row-level linked outcome entered this review. I used published scoring agreement and the prior's reported comparison conclusions to evaluate claims. The sizing scenarios and stable-quality example used no cohort effect values. The prior pin came from the lab's saved metadata manifest. I checked the public readme and repository landing page, which reports CC-BY-4.0, and did not certify item-level licensing, joins, timestamps, blinding or outcome separation. The conclusions apply to the sources inspected.
