# Additional calibration versus a nonlinear future updater

November 5, 2026, response to the PI's same-assignment steering. **This can be a substantive model-use question, conditional on origin-specific reference fidelity and discrimination. The strongest original growth has now been recovered; do not fund a large descriptive grid yet.** More observations improving forecasts is already expected. The consequential uncertainty is whether another 800 early behavioral observations can substitute for a nonlinear future updater when forecasting the **same future acquisition observations**, and where that substitution fails. A positive, precise negative, or a well-resolved interaction would alter whether to invest in early state measurement, neural future dynamics, or both. Merely plotting more prefix/horizon means would add little to the old benchmark.

The primary priors verified in PRIOR_CHECK.md include conditional future/animal generalization and generated decisions, but the accessible descriptions do not establish this matched future-target information tradeoff against the recovered strong growth prescription. This bounded prior check earns a question, not a priority claim. The current common-state experiment supplies no additional-calibration axis: every actor has the same 1,000-opportunity neural prefix estimate. It is evidence about incremental future updates, with neural estimation cost retained, not evidence that an ordinary end-to-end model has learned equally from an extra prefix.

The smallest meaningful primary design is two observation cutoffs and two forecast windows, with three prespecified model prescriptions. For example, calibrate through opportunity **200 or 1,000**, then score the identical future windows **1,001–1,500 and 1,001–3,000**. The early forecast must generate opportunities 201–1,000 as well as the scored window; it cannot assimilate those actual intervening outcomes, adaptive stimuli or rewards. The later forecast may assimilate all legitimate information through 1,000. All 64 current complete-session projections reach opportunity 3,000, so this proposal does not need additional evaluation rows. It explicitly tests waiting for more early calibration versus forecasting earlier, including the changed lead time; it does not identify a biological learning-stage effect or a pure information-amount effect with all other quantities held fixed.

The primary models should be the unchanged neural rule, the recovered strongest ordinary growth/state prescription, and one credible adaptive state/history/engagement prescription which has a defensible generative process. Match source fitting opportunity and validate the 1,000-cutoff ordinary reference before building a short-prefix adaptation. A model name or a source competitive one-step score is insufficient. The common-neural-state growth ablation should be a **separate component sensitivity**: it asks whether neural future updates add value after paying for neural prefix estimation. It cannot support a low-cost independent ordinary-family claim.

Use a proper score for the predeclared accuracy-ratio target, with explicit zero-count/missingness treatment and count/omission diagnostics. Preserve expected particle ratios and count-pooled summaries separately. Fix the model-use margins before a new evaluation, for example 2 pp individual accuracy-error benefit and a 10% proper-score benefit; justify the final values against actual utility rather than selecting a favorable score. Define G(m,h) as ordinary-minus-neural risk on a shared future window. A substantive substitution finding needs a material early G, a smaller late G with an upper bound excluding useful residual benefit, and a directly estimated paired contrast G(200,h)-G(1000,h). A persistent material G after the longer prefix supports future-update utility. A precise small G at both cutoffs would restrict usefulness across the tested information range. A large calibration improvement shared by all models, without an informative model-by-information contrast, does not answer the question.

Keep whole mice and sites as units, and compute paired risk/interaction uncertainty on the same target observations. Four cells per mouse are not four animals.
