**Reliability and scientific payoff.** Reliability of this matched computational attribution is high. It eliminates a concrete ambiguity in the original three-role comparison: learned reliability did not produce the large blocked gain under the retained inference choices. The stronger inference package can succeed without a supplied noise-location flag or adaptive discounting on all tested curricula. This is a restricted constructive result, with a chosen generator and response ceiling near98.2%from the fixed link; it says nothing about human calibration or a unique biological mechanism.

The more consequential surviving hypothesis is that uncertainty about contexts can be converted prematurely into an estimate of irreducible noise, creating curriculum-dependent errors. Full-versus-ablation differences offer a falsifiable model contrast. They do not show that reliability inference is intrinsically harmful, that the original task's random transition is unnecessary for people, or that one must attain98%human performance. The ablation tests one weighting prescription, with endogenous hard assignments, fair-noise and story assumptions and fixed published parameters. The20seed intervals exclude untested generators, parameter uncertainty, participant/cohort variation and model-selection exposure. Original development/scored outcomes and the publication's qualitative findings were already inspected; all new comparisons are postvalidation diagnostics.
