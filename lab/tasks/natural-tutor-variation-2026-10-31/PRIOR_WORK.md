# Prior work and source scope

Primary dataset: Nicholson, Queen and Sober, *Bengalese Finch song repository*, DOI10.6084/m9.figshare.4805749.v9, CC BY4.0. Metadata/source archive/documentation are retained locally. Adult baseline undirected song, selected automatically recorded bouts, human segmentation/labels. Labels incorporate sequential context and are arbitrary across birds. The source does not contain tutor–pupil pairs, natural error judgments, reinforcement outcomes or evidence that every observed rendition is a learned target. The present use of adult repeats as example targets is computational.

Primary methods verification: [Mandelblat-Cerf & Fee2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0096484) already treats variable imitation, automated segment selection, acoustic similarity and sequence similarity. It addresses unreliable syllable/gap segmentation by comparing continuous song. Our human-labelled short-phrase assay does not improve that recognition problem and should not claim first sequence evaluation.

[Zandberg et al.2024](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012329) validates similarity methods against avian perceptual decisions and learns a comparison from those judgments. It describes linear timing tolerance in SAP and flexible dynamic alignment in Luscinia. Thus learned song similarity, timing tolerance and their validation are established topics. Repeated natural examples alone cannot identify the bird's perceptual or corrective objective; this archive supplies no such judgments.

A second search also found [Meliza, Keen & Rubenstein2013](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.4812269), dynamic time warping for natural avian calls; [Anderson, Dave & Margoliash1996](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.415968), template/DTW recognition; and [Luscinia's original documentation](https://luscinia.sourceforge.net/page26/page16/page16.html), which discusses weighting time during alignment. These are primary leads surfaced in search, not full source reproductions. The current assay uses ordinary statistics/alignment; no priority or model-class novelty claim.
