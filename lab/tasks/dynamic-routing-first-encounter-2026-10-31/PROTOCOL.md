## Models and fitting

Four nested logistic response models with five stimulus intercepts and past-only states, implemented exactly in analysis.py:

- L: stimulus-specific response and delivered-reward EWMAs with alpha0.1/0.3, previous same-stimulus response/reward, and elapsed time since that stimulus.
- G: L plus previous global response/reward, global response/reward EWMAs at both rates, and reward recency.
- C: G plus opposite-target response/reward histories, signed target-reward traces and most recently rewarded target, and sinusoidal600s-block schedule features anchored by the first actually rewarded target. This is the strong cheap competition/schedule account.
- R: C plus one shared Bayesian context feature. Two contexts start equally likely; symmetric switch hazard0.01per trial. Delivered reward to a target gives likelihood0.95when its modality is active versus0.05otherwise; a target response with no reward gives the reverse likelihood; no response/no reward gives no evidence. Current target's posterior match is the additional feature. This is an assumed predictive prescription, not a certified mouse rule. C can already use the same cross-stimulus observations without this nonlinear state.

Fit each family on development noninstruction/nonopto/nonrepeat/noncatch rows. All rows update observable histories. Select its ridge strength from0.1,1,10,100 by equal-block mean leave-one-entire-block-out log loss across all eligible development trials; ties choose larger ridge. Standardize using training rows only; intercepts included in feature matrix, ridge exempts the global constant. Final refit on all eligible development trials, then immutable parameters. Logistic probabilities clipped to[0.001,0.999] for forecasting/scoring. No development revision is planned; at most one justified revision could be timestamped before evaluation, preserving failure.

## Endpoint and decision

Retrospective selection uses exported block/instruction labels only: for each block1–5 determine the newly rewarded target from its five instruction stimuli, then select the first noninstruction presentation of the opposite (formerly rewarded) target. Require exactly five same-target instruction rows at the block start, complete earlier chronology, no earlier presentation of that old target within the new block, and no opto/repeat anywhere in that block. Do not skip an ineligible first encounter to choose a later response. No endpoint selected on the response. Save all trial forecasts before computing evaluation losses.

Primary is binary-response log loss at those first encounters, average within each animal then equally across the three animals. Compare R with each L/G/C. A finite practical lead requires mean gain>=0.05nat/encounter and positive gain in every evaluation animal against both G and C; report every contrast even if the rule fails. Also report C–G and G–L to locate generic competition/global utility. Brier loss and observed/forecast response rates are descriptive.
