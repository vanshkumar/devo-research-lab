# What the physical intervention does and does not identify

The ONE selected candidate is delayed visual perturbation feedback followed by an unseen next-trial reach, as implemented in Honda et al. (2012, Frontiers 3:540). Brudner et al. (2016) is the second, endpoint-delay method program used to challenge the proposed timing exclusion. The Honda PLOS companion paper was summary-exposed during discovery; it is not an additional candidate. No target-jump deployment, new person or empirical record is inspected here.

## Source constraints that enter the calculation

Honda adds 0/100/200/300 ms to a reported 60 ms apparatus lag: physical delays are 60/160/260/360 ms. The manipulation is a delayed replay of the hand trajectory with a signed 10-degree rotation. The next trial has no cursor. Its response is the baseline-subtracted direction of the actual hand at peak velocity. The hand is returned only after the delayed cursor stops, followed by a 2 s start hold before the next target. Thus all four visual perturbations precede the next probe. A no-cursor probe excludes its OWN online visual correction, not the preceding trial's visual learning input. Delivery order is not proof of completed neural processing or an exact neural clock.

In Brudner Experiments 1/2, heading uses the line between 1 and 3 cm, before terminal feedback at the 10 cm radius. The cursor is hidden during the outward movement. Error feedback is delayed by 0 or 5 s and displayed for 2 s; the hand must remain at the endpoint until feedback ends, then the robot returns it and a 0.5 s start hold precedes the next target. Nominal lower bounds from terminal crossing to the next target are 2.5/7.5 s, with return and display lag omitted, NOT exact measured clocks. Asking for a reach before the delayed feedback violates this documented hold rule. The published 40-trial no-feedback block does not measure an independently anchored storage variable.

An added target-event/precursor crossing is a proposed modification, not a source-certified condition. Neither article certifies its event-specific error or expression invariances.

## Transition versus fresh error

Grant exact precursor X and programmed physical perturbation U, even though a biological state is not directly measured. A local representation of the learning-driving error is `E=U-c*X`. Then an observed next response is

`Y=a*X+b*E=(a-b*c)*X+b*U`.

Physical randomization of U may estimate its observable response coefficient. It does not make U the internal error or independently identify c. The Jacobian with respect to `(a,b,c)` has null direction `(b,0,1)`: `X*b+(-b*X)*1=0`. Consequently `(a+delta,c)` and `(a,c-delta/b)` match exactly. FIRST_CALCULATION checks both signed perturbations, both supplied precursor directions, and all four source delays, with an independently eliminated-coefficient check. No coefficient is estimated from a paper or person. This is standard reparameterization, not the old bounded-report-bias frontier and not biological evidence for a particular c.

Multiplying both event processing and error correction by a common causal kernel preserves the equality. Before the relevant delayed visual signal arrives, neither signal-driven account can change the response; after it arrives, either can. A minimum sensory latency alone therefore excludes effects of that particular signal before arrival; it does not choose between mechanisms sharing it. An intrinsically altered transition explicitly stipulated to occur before feedback could instead differ from a purely visual-error rival. The timing methods do not establish that stipulation or exclude an error driven by an earlier visible target/event, proprioception or prediction. No numerical biological latency is assumed.

## The favorable contrary succeeds under its stated restrictions

CONTRARY_PREDECLARATION was saved before the second calculation. If delay changes b, while c and intrinsic a are invariant and physical U has known unit loading in E, estimate b from the +/-10-degree contrast and k from the crossed X contrast. Then two different gains give

`c=(k1-k2)/(b2-b1)`, `a=k1+b1*c`.

The exact supplied positive control recovers a=.97 and c=.5 from observable cells, without reading latent coefficients as estimator inputs. This explicitly rejects the stronger claim that delays can NEVER help identification. Releasing c's invariance gives the exact alternative `c'_d=c+(a'-a)/b_d`; all same observable cells and physical-input gains then match. The executed illustrative alternative has c values .15 and -.2; these are local signed loadings, not validated temporal shifts or empirical estimates. Even requiring positive loadings would not generally restore identification: sufficiently small nonzero changes in a preserve positivity for interior c and positive b. No frontier, biologically calibrated loading range or feasibility claim is inferred.

Honda already considers predicted hand position/temporal association and error sensitivity as different contributors. Its restricted hypotheses give different delay profiles; this screen does not refute that comparison. The needed invariance or a known error-integration law would be additional information. Matching the time between trials could control elapsed time but would not certify it.

## Storage versus expression is a separate target

Let the measured effective state be B=S+Z. For the same observable recurrence `B_next=A*B+b*U`, choose any storage coefficient q and set

`S_next=q*S`, `Z_next=A*(S+Z)+b*U-q*S`.

The observed next response remains unchanged. The exact scalar check evaluates 24 supplied cases. It shows a remaining allowed expression representation, not that this is a real biological mechanism. Hand heading at peak velocity, absence of a visual cursor, instructions to stop aiming, and a stable aftereffect do not by themselves identify a loading on S. A storage-specific anchor or independently justified expression restriction is still needed.

## Interpretation and stop

The sources justify intervention timing and operational readouts, with actual limits on what online feedback is available. They do not supply a report-free measurement of the driving error in the proposed event conditions. No empirical outcomes, boundary discrimination, power/coverage, full feasible estimation, target-jump implementation or storage measurement has been tested. The original observable carryover contrast is measurable; only the proposed mechanistic attribution remains unearned. Close cheaply at this standard reference result rather than engineer another arbitrary nuisance class.
