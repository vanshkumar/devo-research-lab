# Collins and Frank association acquisition qualification

**November 5, 2026. Hold confirmation funding.** Collins's original Berkeley release is small enough to reserve in full. A separately packaged Brown EEG experiment gives a credible development cohort and an early window before its performance criterion can terminate a block. The documented deadlines and feedback timing differ. The author releases provide neither an omission calibration bridge nor a certified schedule and omission trace for the Berkeley export. Together these leave the proposed joint generated-response comparison unqualified. The whole-file role assignment is viable as an access design. ## Protocol, timing and information at origin

**Berkeley.** The paper specifies 14 acquisition blocks with fresh stimulus categories, eight at load 3 and six at load 6. First and last have load 3. Each block has a planned 13 × set size opportunities, with 12–14 presentations per image and pseudorandom spacing constrained by the design. That gives 780 planned acquisition opportunities per complete person. Each image has one fixed correct key among three. The task knows this mapping and returns truthful 1/0 feedback after the chosen key. The participant learns the mapping through observed feedback. The stimulus deadline is 1.5 seconds. A timeout produces a distinct message. Reported feedback and fixation each last 0.5 seconds. Methods specify a fixed block length and describe no performance criterion for progression. Actual map sampling, balance and cue ordering, block instructions, omission encoding, possible retries and the mapping from assigned to exported opportunity ordinals remain unverified. The paper's plotted learning curves omit missing answers. [Original methods](https://berkeley-deep-learning.github.io/cs294-131-f18/Collins2018_JoCN.pdf)

**Brown EEG.** The task has the same three action choices, fixed within-image correct key, truthful binary feedback and both loads of interest. Its 22 blocks span loads 1–6, with four at load 3 and three at load 6. Each image appears at least nine times, after which a three-of-last-four criterion can end a block, with a maximum of 15 per image. Thus the first 8 × set size scheduled opportunities precede this stopping condition for uninterrupted blocks. Its deadline is 1.4 seconds, with a 0.1–0.6 second jitter before audiovisual feedback and additional variable feedback and fixation durations. CF12 uses a 2-second deadline, a cumulative performance bar, a criterion and feedback about trials saved. An early cap removes criterion-based censoring within the cap. It does not remove these differences in experience or prior-block practice. [PNAS supplement, protocol](https://ski.clps.brown.edu/papers/CollinsFrank_PNAS_supp.pdf) · [CF12 protocol](https://ski.clps.brown.edu/papers/CollinsFrank_WMRL.pdf)

**Legal inputs.** Freeze the origin in scheduled opportunities. Both forecasts can use the same actual prefix cues, choices, omissions, feedback and available timing, plus the current assignment and any independently preallocated tape available to the forecaster. A taped or verified behavior-independent schedule and map can be given to a common environment. The response policy sees only the current cue, shared instructions and its own generated history after origin. It cannot see the environment's correct key before feedback. Generate each subsequent three-key response or timeout, then apply the verified feedback rule and advance the clock. If only the design law is available, integrate over future cue/order and hidden map draws consistent with the observed prefix. A reconstructed sequence built by filtering on later valid responses is outcome-dependent. The evidence for an independent tape would be the assigned ordinals and mapping plus documented rules that preserve them through timeout or interruption. Checking a constant mapping in the export alone does not establish that law.

**Retention.** The original design inserts an N-back distractor, then repeats each of 54 images four times without feedback, once per quarter. It excludes the first and last acquisition blocks by design. This gives 216 scheduled probes and a possible auxiliary readout of the persistence of acquired mappings. The delayed test was a surprise to the learner. Actual delay, N-back duration and later test participation are future events at an acquisition origin. A model would need to integrate over their law or condition on a genuinely assigned schedule. No-feedback removes corrective reinforcement and allows response dependence or retrieval practice. First probes in eligible blocks are the cleaner auxiliary target. Release of complete test rows and event timing is unverified. Retention is secondary to acquisition. [Original testing protocol](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn_a_01238)

## Closest prior and the decision a test could change

- **Original and exact-record reanalysis.** Collins jointly fits the acquisition and testing phases, compares flexible RL, RLWM and interactive RLWMi, and simulates with each person's fitted parameters. The reward-count, delay, load and block regressions are established alternatives. Li et al. fit the full valid learning choices of each person and compare static noise with a dynamic engaged/lapse process. Their generators filter on factual `choice > 0`, replay the surviving cue sequence, force the first three or six surviving choices, according to block size, to the person's observed choices, then generate later choices and deterministic feedback. The omissions mask changes the clock. Such validation leaves the early-prefix forecast of the joint opportunity-level law open. The scripts were read statically. [Collins modeling methods](https://berkeley-deep-learning.github.io/cs294-131-f18/Collins2018_JoCN.pdf) · [Li et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2024.102842) · [Mask](sources/dne-validate_models.m.txt#L79) · [Forced choices and feedback](sources/dne-dynamic_model.m.txt#L78)
- **Strong family comparators.** McDougle and Collins fit joint choice/RT models on the PNAS study, score held last blocks at each load using within-person fitted parameters, and simulate another study (CF12) with average fitted parameters and that study's block/stimulus sequences. The held-block likelihood uses recorded history for trial updates. The across-study simulation checks common behavioral curves and RT effects. It establishes a serious transfer prior, while leaving individual-prefix personalization against calibrated ordinary history unresolved. Collins's WMH paper also makes a serious reward-insensitive habit alternative. Its validation uses full-record fits and the factual RT mask. The pinned `RLWM.m` copies the input trial matrix, substitutes factual first-iteration choices and updates rows (k-1) through its penultimate retained trial, preserving the last retained choice and reward. No size of any resulting plotting effect was measured. These routines give model references and require new implementation of the legal forecasting boundary. [McDougle and Collins manuscript](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/997154n6) · [WMH paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02340-0) · [WMH fitted simulation](sources/validation.m.txt#L60) · [WMH trial loop](sources/wmh-RLWM.m.txt#L63)
- **Prospective retention prior.** Bera et al. fit joint RT and choice during all learning, then use a common softmax rule on inferred final values to predict unseen two-image reward-value choices. Their test phase is excluded from fitting. That is a genuine phase holdout and makes a generic claim that learning dynamics predict later retention insufficient here. It uses full factual acquisition and a different target. The Rac-Lubashevsky EEG study also uses a related no-feedback association readout, with probabilistic reward magnitude, other loads and full learning/EEG histories. Those are precedents, and neither was nominated as a same-protocol source cohort. [Bera et al., validation methods](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014796) · [Rac-Lubashevsky et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10146488/)

The useful residual is **whether personalizing the update from a short prefix improves the distribution of additional correct-feedback opportunities and omissions on new people, beyond common calibrated history**. An illustrative common window is origin at 2 × set size and horizon at 8 × set size, in predetermined load-3 and load-6 blocks with support in both experiments. That gives 6/12 observed opportunities and 18/36 generated ones. Test candidate block rules against source support for practice, response clock and cue lag. Use paired person-level joint proper loss on remaining correct and omission counts, with equal load weights within people. Preserve the two recruitment batches if identifiable, with one pooled registered primary and batch sensitivity. A material margin would be anchored to an acquisition use, such as the proper-score change corresponding to a one-opportunity difference at the decision horizon, justified and sized using source only. The 10% IBL threshold has no role here. The window and margin rationale are provisional.

The ordinary comparator should predict all three keys and timeout from pooled, regularized counts and recencies of actual chosen/rewarded/failed actions, exposure and lag, block and load, practice, prefix competence, recent omissions and motor stickiness. Truthful binary feedback allows elimination of tested wrong actions and identification after a correct response or two distinct errors. A strong ordinary rule should exploit that information with a lapse/forgetting allowance and source-calibrated overdispersion. Test the personalized updater against this reference and a matched pooled-updater ablation. Keep the recipient's actual prefix, task map, ordinary history and common calibration in both ablation arms. Allow the same lawful prefix RT information when the release supports it. A material benefit on both paired contrasts with satisfactory calibration would justify retaining personal update state for early acquisition forecasts. A tight upper limit with satisfactory ordinary calibration would justify using the pooled predictor in this regime. A gain due only to average calibration or both models' failure after protocol transfer would leave that decision open. The evidence concerns predictive use, with cognitive mechanisms underdetermined.

The Brown source offers up to 280 relevant person-blocks in 40 people before field/completeness checks. Multiple blocks can stabilize a person's loss, and the independent units stay people. ## Whole-object staging and price

Reserve the entire Berkeley version as one evaluation object, with distinct batch strata only if a predeclared post-freeze join supports them. Distinct Brown development leaves no need to split the sealed Berkeley file for source. Under a separate authorized allocation, an isolated source job could take only the exact `Expe3.mat` blob and validate the full row mask, phase/ordinal semantics, feedback/timeouts and source common window. The source model owner sees those rows. A distinct payoff/design reviewer would need supported timing and omission sensitivities, held-source calibration, precision and a frozen computation budget before main funding. After model/design freeze, a separate ingestion role would read the full Berkeley object, record its hash, and apply preregistered identity, phase, chronological opportunity, mapping, omission and completeness checks before releasing any score. Predispositions for the six late-performance exclusions and incomplete histories must be fixed in advance. Preserve all eligible original learners if the release contains them. **Concrete blocker.** Even assuming a common latent RT law, a 1.4-second deadline reveals only the total censored tail above 1.4 seconds. The proportion between 1.4 and 1.5 seconds and its key distribution are unidentified. In Berkeley those responses can yield feedback and change later actions. Reclassifying them as omissions would give the actor the wrong history. Different feedback delays and progression cues add a second transport uncertainty. Published analysis code for both objects uses response-conditioned masks. Raw omission and opportunity fields still require verification. The Berkeley deposit lacks the controller or equivalent documented assignment evidence. A credible bridge could come from an independently packaged matching protocol, or from valid opportunity records plus a conservative, source-supported sensitivity that resolves the decision over plausible deadline and feedback shifts. Those prerequisites would allow a bounded development and timing check. With current evidence a precise CPU/iteration price or useful confirmation power would be speculative. Hold the whole-object main and preserve the byte-qualified route. [Access and reproduction](REPRODUCE.md)
