# A better terminal stimulus does not buy a decisive mouse curriculum test

**November 4, 2026 (ET) · Development decision: do not fund confirmation for this design class.** In 64 consumed source contexts across nine labs, the later same-dose order reversal gave a somewhat clearer predicted contrast at a 25% probe than at exact100. It was too small to discriminate the recurrent forecast from the original growth or fitted reward-history alternatives with 240 independent mice. The simpler early contrast-composition intervention produced the largest nominal disagreement; its best stimulus was rightward exact100. That signal depended on the unchanged recurrent rule, site and engagement. No tested common one-response allocation reached a credible discrimination target across the fitted contenders. This is a conditional design failure, not an empirical null for curriculum or a universal impossibility result.

## What was actually run

The [reference](REFERENCE.json) reduced the original 512-particle exact100 forecasts and consumed accuracy/count summaries on all 64 animals, reproducing the original equal-lab/pooled six-bin curve MAEs: unchanged GRU **5.644/2.080 pp**, fitted session GRU **6.379/0.908**, exposure growth **6.276/1.740**, and persistence **14.408/11.139**. The original equal-lab growth advantage for the unchanged rule is only 0.632 pp. Its last-bin mean forecast bias was −2.56 pp in the earlier 40 and +8.87 pp in the later 24. The pooled session fit's good aggregate does not establish stronger per-site plausibility. Independent scalar update equations on verified saved source endpoints, with generated test steps including a boundary omission, agreed with the frozen vector code to 2.22e−16. This reproduces the archived source forecast reduction and verifies runnable transitions from real source-calibrated contexts. It does not rerun the complete empirical-prefix calibration or the original adaptive path.

The [frozen pilot](PILOT_PLAN.md) used both GRU prescriptions (ρ=1 and fitted ρ=.9) with unchanged parameters and growth forecasts derived from the 58-donor bank. On the matched historical 40 it also used the **actual frozen fitted reward-baseline REINFORCE rule**, with its own saved states and no retuning. It responds to generated stimulus, action and reward. In the original evaluation its high-contrast CRPS was .08289 versus .08097 for growth, and its nested development conditional score was .44538 versus .44887 for the original DNN32. It is a serious simple alternative despite imperfect autonomous calibration. No fitted reward-history state was asserted for the later 24.

Late starts use actual saved joint simulated endpoints at opportunity 4,000, conditioned on the consumed 1,000-trial calibration and source chronology for the previously generated common training. These are predicted states, never measured 4,000-trial states. The early runs use authentic saved 1,000-trial endpoints and marginal coefficient covariance with fixed recurrent memory; the joint coefficient/memory Jacobian was not in those artifacts and was not reconstructed from trial rows. These are conditional approximations for early acquisition, not exact prefix-particle replays. Early growth uses its first saved donor bin; late growth holds its last saved bin. Any arm-blind continuation still predicts a zero causal arm contrast. Nominal omission is shared across models and arms, approximated by smoothed last/first-bin simulated omission counts from the growth draws. This preserves original source context heterogeneity but does not calibrate deployment, terminal omissions, prehistory or retention. No original or cached empirical trial rows, protected inputs or outcomes, new fits, real external policy replay, or new experiment were used. No teacher state or realized postassignment behavior was supplied to forecasts.

Two prospective assignment contrasts were run, each with two fixed 200-slot sessions, fresh per-learner shuffles, exact sign balance within stimulus components, identical trial and session timing, fixed volume per correct response, no adaptive repeat/promotion, and a common final 40-trial distribution. Order reverses 180 easy/180 hard slots with identical exposure counts; composition interleaves a hard-rich or easy-rich schedule. All models generate their own choices, omissions and deterministic correctness feedback. The first terminal response is measured before its own update at a common stimulus drawn independently of the arm and behavior. Correct share counts omissions as failures. Training reward and immediate response state can differ despite equal slots; the design would require apparatus, timing and engagement checks to interpret any real effect as retained acquisition. The [methods prior](METHODS_PRIOR.md) establishes that easy-to-hard schedules and design optimization for model discrimination are prior work. Neither is a novelty claim here.

## The discrimination result

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Early order | Exact100, equal side | −2.93 | −2.14 | 0.00 | −0.37 |
| Early composition | Exact100, equal side | +5.70 | +3.48 | 0.00 | −0.76 |
| Early composition | 25%, equal side | +3.95 | +2.25 | 0.00 | −0.71 |
| Later order | Exact100, equal side | −3.49 | −3.60 | 0.00 | −0.78 |

- **The order effect survives source aggregation, but the measurement gain is modest.** At 25% the later order gaps against reward-history are 3.21 and 2.97 pp; unchanged vs fitted recurrent differ by 0.24 pp. The unchanged 25% contrast is negative in every represented lab, −3.94 pp with leave-one-site range [−4.14,−3.84], and −3.93 in the later 24 alone. Paired MCSE is 0.094 pp; disjoint halves give −3.92 and −3.96. On all 64 contexts the unchanged rule's signed contrasts are +0.45 and −8.24 pp at ±25%, versus +0.37 and −7.31 pp at ±100%. The left/right asymmetry is real in these forecasts, so symmetric averaging loses signal. It may reflect source acquisition asymmetry or immediate choice state; it is not established sensory transfer. Exact best-side point tests at N=240 on the historical mixture give at most 37.0% power against growth and 29.9% against reward-history for unchanged GRU; the fitted GRU gives 34.3% and 27.3%. Equal-side 25% versus reward-history reaches 13.7% and 12.5%.
- **The simpler composition contrast has more nominal signal, at the conventional stimulus.** Early unchanged GRU predicts +5.33 pp on all 64 at balanced exact100 (+9.24 right, +1.43 left); fitted session gives +3.14 (+3.81 right, +2.48 left). In the matched historical 40, allocating all probes to rightward exact100 gives at most **47.0/52.1%** ideal binary power for unchanged versus growth/reward-history and **17.2/20.6%** for the fitted alternative. Balanced exact100 gives only 23.4/27.5% and 13.8/16.7%, respectively. No weaker probe beats the right exact100 point test here. Early unchanged exact100 predicts −8.16 pp at the singleton Dan site and +4.62 to +8.06 elsewhere in the full roster. Excluding the singleton increases the mean but does not support a new-site sign guarantee. Later composition largely loses the early signal. These facts favor the simpler teacher contrast as a development diagnostic, not as a funded main test.
- **The measurement and model assumptions matter at the scale of the gaps.** Making difficult training trials 10 pp more likely to be omitted reduces the early historical exact100 contrast from +5.70 to +4.20 for unchanged and +3.48 to +2.17 for the session fit. Under nominal conditions the early hard-rich unchanged forecast earns about 18 fewer correct rewards across 400 slots than easy-rich; with the omission stress this rises to 28. This is a plausible water, arousal or fatigue pathway absent from the actor. Allowing a 3 pp terminal arm omission perturbation, clipped at feasible probabilities, moves the late 25% unchanged order contrast between −5.31 and −2.52 pp and the reward-history contrast between −1.99 and +0.53 pp. Those are sensitivity ranges, not empirical bounds. A 3 pp contrast calibration band already covers several entire signed vectors, including stressed early composition for the fitted rule versus growth (2.74 pp maximum on the historical roster). Matching omissions and looking only at responses would require checking engagement selection, not simply deleting omissions. The session comparison is fitted-model sensitivity, not a posterior over possible recurrent rules.

### Bound for every common allocation in this small class

To test new teacher prediction rather than accidental baseline calibration, each pair was allowed a shared arm-pooled correct rate for a stimulus/site/context, retaining its own arm difference and the common omission probability. This constructs a concrete calibration rival; it is not a claim that true baseline rates are known or identical. For two categorical first-response laws P,Q, affinity is A = Σ√(P Q), with P and Q covering correct, wrong and omission. Independent affinities multiply, giving power ≤ .05 + √(1 − exp(−2Nd)), capped at one, where d is the average negative log affinity over balanced arms and the fixed source mixture. A common probe mixture averages d, so it cannot beat the best signed cell for that pair; allowing each observed site or even each archived preassignment context its own best cell only raises the ceiling. The iid source-mixture check reaches the same decisions. This is an upper bound for a size-.05 test of the specified pair, not an estimate of its power or a general theorem about curricula.

On the matched 40, maximizing across **both starts, both curricula and both engagement scenarios** gives these upper ceilings at N=240:

| Pair with shared baseline calibration | Best common probe, site known | Optimistic probe tailored to known source context |
|---|---:|---:|
| Unchanged GRU vs growth | 90.0% | 91.3% |
| Unchanged GRU vs reward-history | 90.9% | 92.2% |
| Fitted session GRU vs growth | **72.5%** | **73.9%** |
| Fitted session GRU vs reward-history | **72.9%** | **75.5%** |
| Unchanged vs fitted session GRU | 48.8% | 52.4% |

The unchanged-rule bounds are loose and leave open a narrowly favorable test under ideal site/context assumptions. They do not establish its power. The fitted session rule is an actual source alternative, and the more favorable tailored ceiling against growth is also 73.9% on all 64. Thus there is no robust 80%-power discrimination against the simple forecast family within these schedules/probes under the matched-baseline sensitivity, even before uncertainty about deployment, unobserved sites and selection. This ceiling also applies to mixing the two tested interventions as separate comparisons with those same calibrated arm pairs and fixed roster weights. It does not cover different teacher policies, selecting a special target site/population, learning from the training trajectories as outcomes, stronger context information, or repeated probe responses. The uncentered site-known bounds often reach one: rejecting a fixed baseline level is easier and is not evidence about the teacher contrast.

### Precision, consequence and reopening evidence

Known-effect/no-effect controls at N=240 give exact size 5%, **46.2%** power for a 10 pp true arm difference and **93.0%** for 20 pp, near a 50% mean. Independent one-binomial tails agree within 7.8e−16. At N=64 these powers are 19.9% and 48.5%. Under the favorable symmetric 10 pp construction, 618 independent learners are needed for 80% one-sided power; near 50% a 95% contrast interval at N=240 has a half-width of about 12.7 pp, and 80% power for a ±10 pp equivalence conclusion near a true null needs about 857 (normal approximation). These are planning controls, not claims of feasible biological sample size. At the already generous 240 ceiling (four hypothetical sites of 60), fixed training would consume 336,000 opportunities including the common 1,000-trial early prefix, or 1,056,000 through the later start; these exclude shaping, incomplete visits and losses. Matching sites, maintaining fixed opportunities and observing a probe on every assigned animal have no certified operational costs or feasibility.

A precise and much larger randomized effect would falsify specific small-effect forecasts, but would not validate the GRU merely because growth predicts zero. A convincing positive would have to land near a prospectively separated recurrent contrast and outside the plausible simple/observation envelope on independently assigned learners. A precise null would reject a prespecified large-effect forecast; at this sample size a null-compatible outcome will usually leave these small-effect contenders unresolved. A measured omission or arousal effect alone does not establish acquisition. Reopen only with an actually feasible signed-contrast assignment and terminal measurement, validated preassignment state and timing, bounds on differential omissions/reward engagement and site shifts, and a forecast separation large enough for a meaningful positive **and** precise negative. Near .5 this means roughly 16 pp separation at N=240 even for favorable one-sided point discrimination, or a defensible design with more independent information. A longer assay that uses multiple probe responses must model and validate the probe's learning effects or use independent cohorts; counting repeated trials as unchanged measurements would overstate precision. No main tranche is proposed.

**2026-11-04 — First-response curriculum design**
- Observation: The 64 saved source forecast ensembles supported an actual post-prefix intervention pilot without opening trial rows. A later same-dose order reversal modestly increased terminal discrimination at 25% vs exact100, but larger early composition signals came from one side and changed with the fitted session rule, omission and site.
- Action: Before buying a first-response experiment, test signed probes against a source-fitted history-responsive rule, strip arm-pooled calibration from the comparison, and check the attainable one-response power across fitted transition laws. A better probe within an underpowered class is not a reason to fund it.
- Confidence: high for the declared conditional class and calculations; no biological calibration claimed.
