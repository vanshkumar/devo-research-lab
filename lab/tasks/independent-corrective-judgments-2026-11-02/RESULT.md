## Source meaning and closest prior

[Zandberg2024](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012329) measures trained left/right categorical similarity. Either probe choice is rewarded; a choice is not a recorded judgment that a produced song should be improved toward that side. The paper already learns perceptual embeddings from these choices and compares them with tuned acoustic distance and existing tools, reporting a2.9percentage-point best-model increment over tuned Luscinia. That published comparison was inspected, not numerically reproduced here. Its metric-learning claim is existing work; these rows add no production/correction endpoint to it.

[Carouso-Peck/Goldstein2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30713105/) already reports learning differences under contingent versus yoked video feedback. Its[original deposit](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/63db6zc5fm/2) supplies subject/session aggregates, not vocalization-level acoustics and directional quality judgments. The indexed author-thesis protocol describes song-triggered playback and suppression of new triggers while a video plays. Full publisher/thesis text retrieval failed, so exact deployed trigger/quality semantics are not independently certified. No causal-effect reproduction is claimed. A final similarity endpoint and playback contingency do not by themselves label which vocal feature should be corrected.

## Investment decision

The strongest alternative survives: learned perceptual similarity is already treated by the closest paper, while contingent social feedback can concern timing/engagement rather than an independently measured feature-specific target. These two sources cannot support the proposed comparison of a correction objective against ordinary similarity/calibration/density without adding unobserved target semantics. Joining the unrelated experiments would not create the missing measurement.
