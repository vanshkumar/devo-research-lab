# Original acquisition and strategy evidence

Groblewski et al. (2020) describe an automated stage progression after head fixation and water scheduling. Their protocol starts with a 15-minute session pairing grating orientation changes with noncontingent water, then uses a 60-minute static-grating task with contingent rewards. The subsequent flashed grating and natural image tasks have 250 ms presentation and 500 ms gray intervals. Go trials deliver a change, whereas catch trials measure licking during a corresponding no-change interval. The paper's early grating protocol draws catch with nominal probability 0.25 and limits runs to three of the same type. The stage table specifies a longer response window for Stage 1 than for the flashed stages. These are published protocol settings. Per-session NWB task configuration and controller version would still be needed to validate a generator. [Publisher, Methods](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2020.00104/full) · [Bibliographic pin](acquisition_prior_bibliography.json)

For the early stages the paper describes a two-of-three recent-session advancement rule based on the maximum rolling 100-trial d-prime reaching 2. Allen's release documentation separately describes a d-prime threshold above 1 on two of three days for the imaging transition. The handoff gates, protocol revisions, omissions, auto-rewards and actual session termination need their own provenance before treating the realized future schedule as generable from a prefix. The original acquisition paper discusses training dropouts due to health and later imaging for some of its animals. Its data-availability statement gives an author-request route. That study demonstrates an enrollment frame beyond imaging among its studied animals, without an authorized public entrant-to-trial map in the named release. [Publisher, progression and data availability](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2020.00104) · [Allen transition documentation](sdk_visual_behavior_optical_physiology.rst#L107)

Piet et al. (2024) used PsyTrack empirical-Bayes dynamic logistic regression to characterize visual comparison and timing strategies. Their validation classified lick-bout initiation per image interval by cross-validated ROC AUC against a static model. Weights and hyperparameters were fit per session. This supports the plausibility of time-varying strategy, with observations and regressors from the same sessions. A prospective first-grating acquisition forecast with generated future behavior/feedback and common calibration addresses a different prediction target. The prior supplies a motivation and a strong design comparator, without source performance or precision for that forecast. [Piet et al., Fitting the strategy model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11156560/) · [Original abstracts](prior_two_abstracts.json)
