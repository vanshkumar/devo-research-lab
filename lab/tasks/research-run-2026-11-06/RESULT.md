## Prior and changed decision

Namazova et al. separately measure predictive likelihood on factual histories and compare aggregate optimal-choice curves when the actor's choices select the next rewards from the original participant-indexed tapes. Their actor can observe its own selected rewards, and the fixed-tape comparison measures performance in that environment. Wilson et al. test the first-choice exploration effect. Binz and Schulz's Horizon regret calculation uses probabilities on factual histories. The present result concerns the evidential role of an aggregate rollout criterion for earned-feedback interventions. It does not test Centaur, reproduce the fixed-tape setting, or establish a unique human update rule. [Nearest published implementation](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07887v1#A1.SS3) · [Wilson et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5635655/) · [Prior reconstruction](../../reviews/horizon-feedback-validation-result-2026-11-06/RESULT.md#tape-dependence-and-scientific-decision)

For a feedback intervention, require an explicit check of the simulator's next choice under controlled first-reward innovations at matched origins, with a reward law verified for the environment of use. Include a trajectory check such as terminal switching. Keep the aggregate optimality curve as a performance measure. This changes actual model selection on this benchmark. The original per-position working screen accepts the blind threshold policy and fails the responsive ordinary policy. The innovation and trajectory checks separate them. Where tapes are reused, compare at the appropriate tape or independent reward unit and justify any uncertainty calculation from that support. ## Investment decision on the published tapes

The pinned train header contains one obtained reward per trial and no two-arm reward pair. The published rollout selects between `reward_H` and `reward_I` on each free trial. The saved repository listing identifies one 3,705,037-byte mixed timeline blob and no certified source-only export or row projection. The separate `rw_horizon_data.csv` listing supplies no role or two-arm field manifest. Per-participant generated outputs contain selected simulated payoffs and their own outcome exposure. A client-side filter of the mixed timeline would first acquire the protected body. I found no valid source-only route in the existing evidence. [Pinned inputs](../horizon-feedback-forecast-2026-11-06/ACCESS.md) · [Repository metadata](../horizon-feedback-forecast-2026-11-06/namazova_tree.json) · [Rollout code](../horizon-feedback-forecast-2026-11-06/prior_rollout.py)

The factual-label replay already fixes the better arms when scoring the witness's source choices. Since its decisions ignore the free reward, that optimality calculation would be identical on any fixed tape with the same origins and arm labels. Tape values matter for the witness's actual payoffs, the responsive comparator's choices and the prior's full 31-person evaluation. An authenticated source-only tape could extend the claim to the prior's environment. A pass there would strengthen that extension and a failure would bound it. Neither outcome changes the present demonstration that the aggregate screen admits a feedback-blind policy on the released roster under the tested reward laws. Nor would a fixed-tape aggregate pass itself verify response to a controlled change in reward. The measured 9% leaves room for more work but does not resolve the access, provenance or shared-tape uncertainty. I declined a mixed-body release or a new tape search on that marginal return. A future exact-tape comparison would require an authorized version-linked export with both arm values and certified source roles, plus a frozen two-actor test and distinct result challenge.
