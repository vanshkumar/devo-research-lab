# ArgRewrite V.2 feasibility — close the source-only prospective test

**Decision, November 4 Eastern.** The pinned release supports a linked, pre–Draft 3 measure of how an edit corresponds to a specific piece of first-draft feedback. It does not certify a current-quality and prior-gain comparator available at that time. The scored-draft workbook also pools learners by condition, with no separate learner-level outcome object that could be opened for development while keeping the rest undecoded. Close this allocation before main fitting. The result concerns the available measurement and forecast design. The incremental predictive effect is unresolved.

## Closest prior and investment consequence

- **Cromp and Litman (2021), §§3–4.** On these same 86 essays, Draft 1→2 document TF-IDF similarity correlated with contemporaneous average rubric change at reported \(r=-.219\), and aligned sentence similarity controlling revision count at \(r=-.272\). This establishes the direct relevance of ordinary text-change features as a comparator. Both sides of that analysis concern the same transition. The decision left open is whether additional coding of a response to the *actual* feedback earns its collection cost for forecasting a later revision once current quality and recent gain are measured. A contemporaneous association or use of retrospective baseline scores cannot decide that. [Cromp and Litman](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3051/UGR_6.pdf)
- **Afrin et al. (CHI 2021), §6 Table 3.** The C-versus-A normalized Draft 1→3 gain contrast is .18−.06=.12 and the Draft 2→3 contrast is .08−.05=.03. The paper reports an insignificant omnibus test, a significant C–A pairwise comparison for the longer window, and no significant pairwise gain contrast during the actual interface window. These contrasts have different denominators. One cannot attribute their arithmetic difference to the interface or to a particular response history. A lagged forecast would address measurement choice for a subsequent study. It would leave the interface's causal effect and the reported survey outcomes as separate claims. [Afrin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.07018)
- **Litman et al. (AIME-Con 2026), §§3.3–4.2.** The three-draft eRevise comparison already uses assessment of first-feedback implementation to select revision guidance, studies contemporaneous survey/outcome associations and randomizes students within teacher to two systems. Its reported text-evidence gain differs by task, with outcomes mainly scored by the systems' own NLP measures, selection on complete data and uncorrected comparisons. A separate lagged comparison against current score, ordinary history and prior gain would inform whether a future collection should measure implementation as an incremental forecast feature. This ArgRewrite cohort cannot reverse the eRevise treatment result or license an allocation of help. [Litman et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2026.aimecon-main.6/)

**Smallest conditional reopening.** Obtain a legitimate timestamped, draft-isolated Draft 1/2 rubric series with an independent or order-blind Draft 3 score and a sealed keyed target release. A protocol that scores stored Draft 1/2 texts while blind to Draft 3 could define a *new* as-of-Draft-2 assessment protocol, which would need a separately justified and authorized scoring allocation. Also require a development uptake feature with support across writers and a documented decision about paying for that additional coding. Compare one frozen regularized forecast of Draft 3 score with and without the incremental feature. Use the Draft 2 rubric profile, Draft 1/2 gain, ordinary text change and revision purposes, feedback demand/opportunity and eligible interface/cohort history in both models. Include persistence and mean-change references, paired writer loss and a feedback-link disruption control that preserves comparable feedback opportunities. Measure the additional coding cost against the actual measurement decision before fixing any useful margin. A single common-prompt holdout would address within-design increment only. A new prompt, deployment or rater regime would require separate evidence.

There are at most **62** raw-unopened writer keys after development, with at most 14/16/16/16 by arm. Neither 258 drafts nor hundreds of sentence/rubric records add independent forecast targets. An optimistic iid normal approximation gives a 95% mean paired-loss interval half-width of `1.96/sqrt(62) = 0.249` paired-loss SD, or 0.49–0.52 SD within one arm before model, rater or selection uncertainty. Thus the source might discriminate a large pooled gain under a new valid measurement protocol, with limited support for moderate subgroup gains or negative equivalence. Without that protocol, no further current stage is funded. Conditional planning estimates if valid archived scores and a selective release already exist are 1–2 worker hours for provenance/splitting, 2–4 for a narrow baseline and feature development, 1–2 for a single frozen read and uncertainty, and 2–4 for distinct challenge plus one repair/closeout, all with sub-GB local files and minute-scale fits. If new dual human ratings were necessary, 516 draft ratings at an assumed 5–10 min each alone cost about 43–86 hours before uptake coding, adjudication or review. These are planning ranges, not measured use or authorized work.
