# Why the proposed curriculum experiment cannot distinguish the forecasts

**PI assessment, November 4, 2026 Eastern.** For the proposed 40% versus 55% medium-contrast mouse curriculum and six-response assay, near-perfect recovery of a forecast law from simulated paths is not evidence that the proposed 240-animal study can distinguish the models' predictions about what the curriculum changes. This is a consequential, independently challenged experiment-design result. Do not collect this cohort to adjudicate the two recurrent acquisition forecasts against a calibrated simple tracker. Biological acquisition transport remains unresolved.

**What earns that conclusion.** The [investigation](../acquisition-reactive-sequence-2026-11-04/RESULT.md) used exact source-verified measurements from 233,116 already consumed opportunities on 64 mice in nine labs and audited historical model training exposure. The simple tracker updates psychometric state from legitimate past responses, rewards and omissions. Its shared parameters were fit with each test site excluded; the frozen recurrent actors shared fit sites with 35 of the 64 animals. The conditional ordinary-training comparison supports the tracker and component stimuli and observed feedback patterns, not equal validation of the recurrent weights or actual transport to the proposed assignment. The exact candidate did not occur in the ordinary calibration windows. The calibrated simulation generated behavior, rewards and omission histories, including the feedback updates during the six common probe trials, without factual postassignment inputs. It compared the candidate with both an ordinary and a composition-matched reordered sequence of equal length and with the terminal first response. Planted later effects were recovered without giving the first response extra signal.

At 240 independent learners, tests of raw full paths against the tracker gave .997–1.000 power. At a shared arm-pooled trajectory distribution the candidate gave .054–.064 minimum directional nominal power against the two recurrent prescriptions and no material gain over either length-matched control. The [independent challenge](../../reviews/acquisition-reactive-sequence-2026-11-04/REVIEW.md) did not import the author scoring code. It stripped assignment labels; power was still 99.78–100%, with at least 99.89% of raw candidate separation available without knowing assignments across the checked pooling, site and prefix cases. The path marginal is postassignment and can include effects averaged over arms; it cannot by itself establish discrimination of the assigned difference. The reviewer then used a different common-profile calibration that preserves every additive arm difference in path probability. Over the stated class and the most favorable four-site panel with true archived prefixes revealed, the smaller directional power ceiling still reached at most .488. This is a bound for the saved laws, assumed common profiles and cohort scale, not a universal power limit. The independent method also recovered a planted additive contrast. Finite integration, omission miscalibration and site transport limit smaller residual interpretations.

**What changes.** The uncalibrated test would make roughly 576,000 baseline and training opportunities look like a decisive investment; the supported contrast-specific comparison does not. Use model recovery without assignment labels as a diagnostic, and price power for the predicted assigned-arm difference across defensible response calibrations before choosing a cohort. If the question instead concerns a large average cumulative correctness effect, the ordinary six-slot assay gives essentially the same modeled precision as the engineered sequence (roughly 4–5 pp half-width at 240, versus about 12 pp from the first response). The two measurements have different purposes: the first captures immediate response before its own feedback; the sequence captures an evolving response trajectory. Neither should be described as six independent observations of unchanged competence.

The closest model-discrimination design literature already optimizes experiments for identifying competing response models and studies robustness. No new KL theorem or general design priority is claimed. The new scoped contribution is a source-anchored, cost-relevant counterexample to using raw joint model recovery as the readiness criterion for this assigned curriculum comparison, with an independent test ruling out the main concern that one matching prescription removed the arm effects. It changes the next research decision. Further tuning the tested orders, fitting on consumed outcomes, or running the proposed contrast at this scale will not address the supported problem. Reopen the primary biological comparison for a legitimate independently assigned curriculum with matching acquisition and common-probe measurements that constrain engagement and reward history and support a materially larger arm-specific contrast. Future evidence must separate agreement on overall responses from discrimination of the curriculum effect.
