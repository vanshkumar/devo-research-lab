## Question, competing answers and scientific scope

Can variable but correct demonstrations supply enough geometry for complete scalar-feedback correction when motor actions mix meaningful and harmless sensory changes? Does a learned finite-support evaluator earn a material gain over the strongest simple distribution account? The strongest alternative is that ordinary regularized covariance captures the useful information; apparent failure of a template reflects the chosen actuator tradeoff. This is an abstract aligned-feature computational design, neither a bird-song model nor a neural-circuit reproduction. No architecture/novelty claim.

The six sensory coordinates may be called three content and three timing features, but their values have no acoustic units or calibrated biological meaning. All coordinates/correspondences are supplied. Harmless variation is DEFINED to be a two-dimensional Euclidean disk, radius2, embedded at unknown center mu and unknown orthonormal plane U. Tutors are128iid samples uniform on that disk plus independent isotropic Gaussian measurement error sigma=.08. The disk is the entire acceptable performance set. Rank2, uniform-disk/isotropic-noise family and sigma are supplied equally to distribution/geometry methods; no true center, plane, radius, clean tutor, nuisance label per rendition or motor model is supplied. Templates may ignore priors. Radius2 is known to the generator/oracle only; learned disk estimates it from sample covariance. Calling the data variation harmless, adopting Euclidean feature units, and treating finite support as the corrective objective are substantive purchased assumptions, not learned biological facts. The distribution's likelihood and its support-distance are different objectives.

For each whole independently seeded task, U and complement V come from random orthogonal basis; Q is independent4Dorthogonal, C entries iidN(0,.8²). Four motor actions a in[-1,1]^4 produce x=mu+Uz*+(VQ+UC)(a-a*), a* iiduniform[-.6,.6]^4, norm(z*)=.85R. All parameters are independently drawn as run.py states. The plant is deliberately chosen so the true disk is reachable but its center generally is not. True error is dist(x,disk)^2/4, including excess radius. Actor never sees these hidden quantities.
