## Empirical calibration and evaluation

1. Use the two main Zhao pooled element-duration medians as descriptive calibration only. The two-parameter affine map has zero calibration degrees of freedom and cannot claim fit evidence. Compare identity, proportional and constant simple baselines. Do not bootstrap elements as birds or convert medians to means.
2. Use the same encoding and practice law across artificial-only and conflicting-natural-tutor conditions. Natural-tutor influence and optogenetic takeover are not identified by two duration medians. Report predictions over takeover strength rather than choosing it after seeing conflict results. Exact complete overwrite predicts the artificial-only endpoint; incomplete overwrite predicts a convex combination. This prediction belongs to all three architectures under common acquisition assumptions.
3. Lesion before acquisition sets acquisition gate to zero; after acquisition removes new input without deleting downstream state. This mapping tests persistent downstream storage across all architectures. Include a separate labile NIf-resident storage variant in which post-acquisition lesions erase state. Do not claim actual lesion is a selective intervention on a comparator.
4. Adult HVC cooling calibration and cooling-only-during-tutoring outcomes are a different intervention. For dimensionless clock speed c_T during tutoring, shared/internal-clock encoding gives stored ticks c_T D; at uncooled practice c_P=1, duration ratio D_pupil/D_tutor=c_T and speed ratio=1/c_T. External-time reference gives ratio 1. Fit no clock sensitivity to the pupil outcome. Require independent temperature/clock calibration and match the exact reported duration/tempo metric; otherwise evaluate direction only and quantify the unmeasured mapping. A stored reference expressed in clock ticks makes the same prediction as shared/motor codes. Do not transfer the artificial affine intercept to natural tutoring without evidence.
